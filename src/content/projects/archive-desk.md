One case per lead. An agent sweeps archives, old newspapers, find registers and old maps,
reads handwriting with two different models and settles every number on an enlarged crop,
keeps a ledger of what each fact rests on, and narrows the question to a box on a map. Then
it looks without touching: a published laser terrain model on land, the
[wreckscan](/projects/wreckscan/) pipeline at sea.

Home ground is Upper Austria. A private map there holds about 5,500 documented sites, 126
finds reported in newspapers between 1850 and 1938, and 233 legends, kept strictly apart.

It never digs, detects or dives. In Austria any search on the spot needs a permit and a
qualified archaeologist, so a finished case ends as a dossier for the heritage office.
Positions of unrecorded sites are not published. Access to the map is on request, by e-mail
to [info@joseftot.com](mailto:info@joseftot.com).
